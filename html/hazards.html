UNDERSTANDING RISK

Two Kinds Of Danger.

Understanding mountain hazards is the first step to a safe summit. Mountaineers usually divide hazards into two groups.

Objective hazards

These come from the mountain itself: avalanches, rockfall, crevasses, storms, lightning and extreme cold. Climbers cannot remove them, but they can reduce their exposure through timing, route choice and speed.

Subjective hazards

These come from the climber: poor fitness, tiredness, weak technique, bad decisions and "summit fever", the urge to keep going when it is time to turn back. Training, honesty and a good team reduce them.

IF SOMETHING GOES WRONG

Emergency Steps.

Stay calm and follow the same order every time. Clear thinking saves more lives than speed.

01

Stop & Assess

Make sure you are not in danger yourself. Check the scene for falling rock, snow or other threats before helping.

02

Protect The Casualty

Move them only if they are in more danger where they are. Insulate them from the ground and keep them warm and dry.

03

Call For Help

Use a phone, radio or satellite device. Give your exact location, the number of people hurt and their injuries.

04

Stay Together

Never leave an injured person alone unless there is no other way to get help. Mark your position so rescuers can see it.

THE SAFEST CHOICE

When In Doubt, Turn Back.

Most hazards become more dangerous as the day goes on. An early start and a firm turn-around time protect you from almost all of them.
