YOU ARE NOT ALONE ON THE MOUNTAIN

Why Join A Club?

Almost every great climber started in a club. Mountaineering clubs and national federations run training courses, lend equipment, organize group trips and keep records of routes and accidents so that everyone can learn from them.

Many also run huts and rescue services, publish journals and guidebooks, and campaign to protect mountain environments. For a new climber, a club is the safest way to find experienced partners.

  • Courses in rock, snow, ice and first aid
  • Group meets and expeditions with experienced leaders
  • Discounts on huts, insurance and equipment
  • A community that shares advice and conditions

SUCCESS STORIES

From Our Organized Camps.

Every season, our camps help climbers take their next step safely. Here are a few of the stories that make us proud.

Skardu, Pakistan · 2025

Glacier School On The Baltoro

Eighteen trainees spent twelve days learning roped glacier travel and crevasse rescue on the approach to K2 base camp. Every participant passed the final rescue drill.

Khumbu, Nepal · 2025

First Steps To 6,000 Metres

A group of first-time high-altitude climbers followed a slow acclimatization plan and reached the summit of a 6,000-metre trekking peak with no cases of serious altitude sickness.

Naltar, Pakistan · 2024

Winter Survival Weekend

Students from three universities built snow shelters, practised avalanche transceiver searches and spent a night in temperatures of −18°C, warm and safe.

Fairy Meadows · 2024

Women's Trekking Camp

A women-only camp below Nanga Parbat introduced navigation, first aid and leave-no-trace skills. Several participants have since joined climbing clubs.

Clean Mountain Expedition

Volunteers and local guides collected rubbish from trekking routes and camps above Hunza, then sorted it for recycling in the valley.
