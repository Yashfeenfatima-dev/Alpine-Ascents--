MEASURING THE MOUNTAINS

Records Tell The Story Of The Sport.

Records in mountaineering are not only about speed or height. They mark the moments when climbers proved something new was possible: the first time a summit was reached, the first ascent without bottled oxygen, the first climb in the depth of winter.

This page collects the most important records: the fourteen peaks above 8,000 metres, the highest mountain on every continent, and the human achievements that shaped climbing history.

Note: heights are rounded to the nearest metre and can differ slightly between surveys. Everest's official height of 8,848.86 m was announced jointly by Nepal and China in 2020.

ONE ON EVERY CONTINENT

The Seven Summits.

Climbing the highest mountain on each continent became a famous challenge after Dick Bass completed the list in 1985.

Asia

Everest

8,849 m

Nepal / China. The highest point on Earth.

South America

Aconcagua

6,961 m

Argentina. The highest peak outside Asia.

North America

Denali

6,190 m

Alaska, USA. Famous for brutal cold and storms.

Africa

Kilimanjaro

5,895 m

Tanzania. A dormant volcano climbed on foot.

Europe

Elbrus

5,642 m

Russia. A double-coned volcano in the Caucasus.

Antarctica

Vinson

4,892 m

The remote, frozen roof of Antarctica.

Oceania

Puncak Jaya

4,884 m

Indonesia. Also called Carstensz Pyramid.

Australia

Kosciuszko

2,228 m

Used in the original "Bass list" instead of Puncak Jaya.

HUMAN ACHIEVEMENTS

Record-Setting Climbers.

Milestones that changed what people believed was possible in the mountains.

First Ascent

First On Everest

Edmund Hillary of New Zealand and Tenzing Norgay of Nepal reached the summit on 29 May 1953.

1953
Women

First Woman On Everest

Junko Tabei of Japan reached the summit on 16 May 1975 and later climbed all Seven Summits.

1975
No Oxygen

All 14 Without Oxygen

Reinhold Messner of Italy became the first person to climb all fourteen 8,000ers, all without bottled oxygen.

1986
Winter

Everest In Winter

Krzysztof Wielicki and Leszek Cichy of Poland made the first winter ascent on 17 February 1980.

1980
Most Summits

31 Times On Everest

Kami Rita Sherpa of Nepal holds the record for the most ascents of Everest, reaching 31 summits by 2025.

31×
Speed

14 Peaks In 92 Days

Kristin Harila of Norway and Tenjen (Lama) Sherpa of Nepal climbed all fourteen 8,000ers in just 92 days.

2023
Pakistan

First Pakistani On K2

Ashraf Aman became the first Pakistani to climb K2, as part of a Japanese expedition.

1977
Pakistan

First Pakistani On Everest

Nazir Sabir, who had already climbed K2 in 1981, became the first Pakistani to reach Everest's summit.

2000
Pakistan

First Pakistani Woman On Everest

Samina Baig of Hunza reached the summit and later completed the Seven Summits.

2013
Age

Oldest On Everest

Yuichiro Miura of Japan reached the summit at the age of 80.

80 yrs
Age

Youngest On Everest

Jordan Romero of the USA reached the summit at the age of 13.

13 yrs
Women

Most Summits By A Woman

Lhakpa Sherpa of Nepal has climbed Everest ten times, more than any other woman.

10×
