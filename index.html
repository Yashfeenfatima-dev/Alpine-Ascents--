WELCOME TO ALPINE ASCENTS

Every Mountain Has
A Story To Tell.

Mountaineering is more than climbing a mountain. It is a journey of preparation, courage, teamwork and respect for nature.

Mountaineering, also called mountain climbing, is the sport of attaining, or attempting to attain, high points in mountainous regions, mainly for the pleasure of the climb.

The word is often used loosely for walking up low hills, but it more properly describes climbing where the terrain and weather are hazardous enough that previous experience is needed for safety. For the untrained, mountaineering can be a dangerous pastime.

Unlike most sports, mountaineering has no single rule book or governing body. Climbers follow a wide variety of techniques and philosophies, and this website brings them together: the history of mountain climbing, essential equipment, climbing techniques, mountain shelters and important safety measures.

A LEGACY OF EXPLORATION

The History
Of Mountaineering.

For centuries, mountains have inspired explorers and adventurers. Over time, mountaineering developed into an activity that combines skill, endurance, planning and technical knowledge.

Modern climbing is usually dated to 1786, when Mont Blanc was first climbed. The sport then moved from the Alps to the greater ranges of the Himalaya and Karakoram, where every one of the fourteen 8,000-metre peaks had been climbed by 1964.

ADVENTURE WITH RESPONSIBILITY

Reach The Summit.
Return Safely.

A successful mountain adventure begins with careful planning, suitable training and respect for changing mountain conditions. Most accidents happen on the way down, when climbers are tired, so the plan must cover the whole journey.

  • Check weather forecasts before departure.
  • Choose suitable routes and train properly.
  • Carry appropriate gear and emergency supplies.
  • Climb with experienced guides when needed.
  • Respect nature and follow local safety advice.
