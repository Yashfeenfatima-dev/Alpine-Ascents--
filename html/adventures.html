BEFORE YOU GO

There Is A Mountain For Everyone.

Mountain adventure covers a wide range of activities. Some need only good boots and a map, while others require ropes, ice axes and months of training.

Start with what suits your fitness and experience today, then build up step by step. Experienced climbers began with simple hikes too.

On this page you will find the main types of mountain adventure, the styles climbers use on big mountains, the climbing disciplines, and a simple guide to choosing the right difficulty.

STYLES OF MOUNTAINEERING

How Big Mountains Are Climbed.

"Style" describes the way a team climbs a mountain: how much support it uses, how it moves and how much it carries.

01 Fast & Light

Alpine Style

A small team carries everything it needs and climbs from bottom to top in one push, without fixed ropes or stocked camps. It is fast and elegant, but leaves little room for error.

02 Supported

Expedition Style

Also called siege style. Large teams fix ropes and set up a chain of camps, moving up and down to carry loads and acclimatize. Most of the first 8,000-metre ascents used this method.

03 In Between

Capsule Style

The team moves as a self-contained unit, fixing a limited length of rope above its camp and then pulling it up to move the "capsule" higher. A balance of speed and safety.

04 Highest Risk

Solo Climbing

One climber moves alone, with or without a rope system. Solo ascents demand complete skill and judgement because there is no partner to help in an emergency.

05 Pure Effort

Without Bottled Oxygen

Climbers rely on acclimatization alone above 8,000 metres. Messner and Habeler first showed it was possible on Everest in 1978, and it remains a serious test of the body.

06 Up And Down

Ski Mountaineering

Climbers skin up on skis with special bindings and ski back down. It is popular in the Alps and is now an Olympic sport, but needs strong avalanche knowledge.

CLIMBING DISCIPLINES

The Skills Inside Mountaineering.

A long mountain route can include several of these disciplines in a single day. Tap each one to read more.

Traditional & Sport Rock Climbing

In traditional (trad) climbing the leader places removable protection such as nuts and cams in cracks, and the second climber removes it. In sport climbing the rock already has fixed bolts, so climbers clip in as they go. Both use dynamic ropes, harnesses and a belayer.

Ice Climbing

Climbers ascend frozen waterfalls or ice-covered rock using two ice tools and crampons. Protection comes from ice screws twisted into the ice. Ice quality changes with temperature, so judgement is as important as strength.

Mixed Climbing

Mixed routes combine rock, ice and frozen turf. Climbers use ice tools and crampons on bare rock as well as ice, a technique called dry tooling. Many hard alpine faces are mixed climbs.

Big Wall Climbing

Huge cliffs such as El Capitan in the USA or Trango Tower in Pakistan can take days to climb. Teams haul bags of food and water and sleep on portaledges, hanging tents fixed to the wall.

Bouldering

Short, powerful climbs on boulders without a rope, protected by crash pads and spotters. Bouldering builds the finger strength and technique that climbers later use on bigger routes.

Via Ferrata

Italian for "iron road". Steel cables, ladders and rungs are fixed to the rock, and climbers clip in with a special lanyard. It gives beginners a safe taste of exposed mountain terrain.

ESSENTIAL GEAR

What To Carry.

  • Sturdy, broken-in hiking or mountaineering boots.
  • Layered clothing: base, insulation and waterproof shell.
  • Backpack with rain cover.
  • Map, compass or GPS, and a headlamp.
  • First aid kit, sun protection and extra food.
  • Helmet, harness and rope for technical climbs.

BEST TIME TO GO

Seasons Matter.

Spring
Melting snow, blooming valleys. Watch for unstable snow and swollen rivers.
Summer
Main trekking and climbing season with longer days. Expect afternoon storms.
Autumn
Clear skies and quiet trails, but colder nights and shorter days.
Winter
Best for snow and ice skills. Needs expert planning and avalanche knowledge.
