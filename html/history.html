A LEGACY OF EXPLORATION

THE HISTORY OF
MOUNTAINEERING.

For centuries mountains were feared. Then, one summit at a time, people learned to climb them, and to climb them safely.

Start The Journey ↓

WHERE IT ALL BEGAN

From Fear
To Fascination.

Before the 1700s, most people avoided high mountains. They were places of danger, weather and legend. Hunters, shepherds and crystal seekers knew them well, but climbing for its own sake was almost unknown.

That changed in the Alps. Scientists wanted to measure and study the peaks, and a few brave climbers wanted to reach the top. Their efforts created the sport we now call mountaineering.

There were earlier hints of the idea. In 1336 the Italian poet Petrarch described climbing Mont Ventoux simply to enjoy the view, and in 1492 Antoine de Ville used ladders and ropes to reach the top of Mont Aiguille in France on the orders of King Charles VIII. These were rare events, and it took almost three more centuries before climbing became a pursuit in its own right.

THE GREAT ERAS

Four Chapters Of Climbing.

Mountaineering history is usually told in four broad periods, each with its own goals, tools and heroes.

1786 – 1854

The Pioneers

Scientists and local guides explore the Alps. Mont Blanc is climbed in 1786 and summits slowly become goals in themselves.

1854 – 1865

The Golden Age

Starting with Alfred Wills on the Wetterhorn in 1854, British climbers and Swiss guides make first ascents of most major Alpine peaks.

1920s – 1964

The Greater Ranges

National expeditions head for the Himalaya and Karakoram. By 1964 all fourteen peaks above 8,000 metres have been climbed.

1970s – Today

The Modern Era

Climbers go lighter, faster and without bottled oxygen. Winter ascents, speed records and commercial expeditions change the sport.

KEY MOMENTS

The Timeline.

Choose an era to see how climbing changed.

  1. 1786

    Mont Blanc Is Climbed

    Jacques Balmat and Michel-Gabriel Paccard reach the summit of Mont Blanc, the highest peak in the Alps. Many historians treat this as the birth of modern mountaineering.

  2. 1854

    The Golden Age Begins

    Alfred Wills climbs the Wetterhorn in Switzerland. His ascent is often used to mark the start of the "Golden Age of Alpinism", when most great Alpine summits were reached for the first time.

  3. 1857

    The Alpine Club Is Founded

    The first mountaineering club forms in London. Climbers begin to share routes, records and techniques, and the Golden Age gathers pace.

  4. 1865

    The Matterhorn Falls

    Edward Whymper's party makes the first ascent of the Matterhorn. Four of the seven climbers die on the descent, a tragedy that shocks Europe and forces a serious conversation about risk.

  5. 1938

    The Eiger North Face

    Anderl Heckmair, Ludwig Vörg, Heinrich Harrer and Fritz Kasparek climb the north face of the Eiger, one of the last great problems of the Alps.

  6. 1950

    The First 8,000er

    A French expedition led by Maurice Herzog climbs Annapurna I, the first of the world's fourteen peaks above 8,000 metres to be summited.

  7. 1953

    Everest Is Climbed

    On 29 May, Edmund Hillary and Tenzing Norgay reach the top of Mount Everest. In the same year, Hermann Buhl makes the first ascent of Nanga Parbat in Pakistan, climbing the final stretch alone.

  8. 1954

    K2 Is Conquered

    An Italian expedition, with Lino Lacedelli and Achille Compagnoni reaching the summit, makes the first ascent of K2 in the Karakoram, the second highest mountain on Earth.

  9. 1964

    The Last 8,000er

    A Chinese team climbs Shishapangma in Tibet. With this ascent, all fourteen of the world's 8,000-metre peaks have been climbed.

  10. 1975

    A Woman On Top Of The World

    Junko Tabei of Japan becomes the first woman to climb Mount Everest.

  11. 1978

    Everest Without Bottled Oxygen

    Reinhold Messner and Peter Habeler climb Everest without supplementary oxygen, proving what the human body can do at extreme altitude. Messner repeats the feat solo in 1980.

  12. 1980

    Everest In Winter

    Polish climbers Krzysztof Wielicki and Leszek Cichy make the first winter ascent of Everest on 17 February, starting an era of Polish winter Himalayan climbing.

  13. 1986

    All Fourteen

    Reinhold Messner completes all fourteen 8,000-metre peaks, the first person to do so. In 2010, Edurne Pasaban becomes the first woman to finish the list.

  14. 2013

    Pakistan's Everest Pioneer

    Samina Baig becomes the first Pakistani woman to climb Everest. Nazir Sabir had become the first Pakistani man to stand on the summit in 2000.

  15. 2021

    K2 In Winter

    On 16 January, a team of Nepali climbers makes the first winter ascent of K2, the last 8,000er to be climbed in the cold season.

  16. 2023

    Fourteen Peaks In 92 Days

    Kristin Harila of Norway and Tenjen (Lama) Sherpa of Nepal climb all fourteen 8,000ers in 92 days, the fastest time recorded.

TOOLS OF THE TRADE

How Gear Changed Climbing.

Better equipment did not remove the danger, but it made careful climbing possible.

Clothing & Boots

Early climbers wore wool, tweed and leather boots with hobnails. Modern layers and insulated boots keep climbers warmer and drier at far greater heights.

Ropes & Protection

Hemp ropes gave way to nylon in the 20th century. Today's dynamic ropes absorb the shock of a fall, and anchors and harnesses protect the climber.

Navigation & Weather

Paper maps and compasses are still essential, but satellite forecasts and GPS now help teams plan safer routes and summit windows.

Oxygen & Medicine

Bottled oxygen helped the first Everest teams, and a better understanding of altitude sickness now saves lives on every big mountain.

THE WORLD ABOVE THE CLOUDS

Pakistan's Giants.

Five of the world's fourteen 8,000-metre peaks stand in Pakistan's Karakoram and Himalaya: K2, Nanga Parbat, Gasherbrum I, Broad Peak and Gasherbrum II.
