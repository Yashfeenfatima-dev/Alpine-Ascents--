A SPORT THAT KEEPS CHANGING

What Is New In Mountaineering.

Mountaineering may be one of the oldest outdoor sports, but it changes every season. Governments update permit rules, engineers design lighter gear, forecasters predict summit windows more accurately and a warming climate reshapes the mountains themselves.

This section collects the most important recent developments, grouped by topic. Use the filters to focus on what interests you, and open any story to read the details.

Last reviewed: October 2026. Rules and fees change often, so always confirm them with official sources before you travel.

LATEST DEVELOPMENTS

News From The Mountains.

Ten developments that are shaping how people climb today.

Rules & PermitsSeptember 2025

Everest Permit Fee Rises To US$15,000

Nepal increased the royalty for foreign climbers on the normal spring route of Everest from US$11,000 to US$15,000, the first rise in about a decade.

Read more

Fees for the autumn and winter seasons went up as well. The government says the extra money will support mountain safety, waste management and the welfare of climbing workers. Debate also continues in Nepal about requiring Everest applicants to have climbed another high Himalayan peak first.

Rules & PermitsSince 2024

Carry Your Waste Off Everest

Climbers on Everest and Lhotse must now use special waste bags above base camp and bring them back down to be checked.

Read more

The rule was introduced by the local rural municipality in the Khumbu region. At high camps, where it is too cold for waste to break down, human waste had become a serious health and environmental problem. Bags are handed out at base camp and inspected on return.

Technology2024 – Today

Drones Over The Khumbu Icefall

Heavy-lift cargo drones have been trialled to carry oxygen bottles, ropes and ladders from base camp to Camp 1, and to fly rubbish back down.

Read more

The Khumbu Icefall is one of the most dangerous sections of Everest, and Sherpa workers cross it many times each season to supply higher camps. Every load a drone carries is one fewer trip through the icefall for a person. Regulators are still deciding how drones should be used on the mountain.

Technology2022 – Today

Satellite SOS In Your Pocket

Many new smartphones can now send emergency messages by satellite, so climbers can call for help far beyond mobile coverage.

Read more

Dedicated satellite messengers have existed for years, but built-in phone features have made the technology available to far more people. Rescue teams warn that a phone is not a plan: batteries die in the cold, and help can still take many hours or days to arrive.

TechnologyOngoing

Lighter, Stronger Gear

Ultralight fabrics, thin high-strength ropes and slings, and lighter crampons and ice tools let climbers move faster with less weight.

Read more

Lighter gear makes alpine-style climbing possible on bigger mountains. The UIAA continues to test equipment such as ropes, harnesses and helmets so climbers can trust that lighter does not mean weaker.

TechnologyMay 2025

The Xenon Debate

A small British team reached the summit of Everest about a week after leaving London, after pre-acclimatizing at home and inhaling xenon gas.

Read more

Supporters say fast "flash" expeditions reduce the time climbers spend in danger. Many doctors and mountain medicine experts, including the UIAA Medical Commission, have warned that there is no good evidence xenon helps acclimatization and that it may carry risks.

EnvironmentJune 2023

Himalayan Glaciers Are Shrinking Fast

A major study by ICIMOD found that glaciers in the Hindu Kush Himalaya could lose up to 80% of their volume by 2100 if emissions stay high.

Read more

Glaciers in the region melted much faster in the 2010s than in the decade before. For climbers this means more rockfall, more unstable icefalls and new glacial lakes that can burst. For nearly two billion people downstream, it threatens water supplies.

EnvironmentEvery Season

Cleaning The World's Highest Peaks

Clean-up campaigns now remove tonnes of rubbish, old ropes and abandoned tents from Everest, K2 and other popular mountains each year.

Read more

Many expeditions must now pay a refundable garbage deposit that they get back only when they bring their waste down. Local climbers and porters lead much of the clean-up work at high camps.

Records & SportMay 2025

Kami Rita Sherpa Reaches 31 Summits

The Nepali guide extended his own record for the most ascents of Mount Everest to 31.

Read more

Kami Rita first stood on top of Everest in 1994 and has guided clients there almost every year since. His record shows the central role Sherpa guides play in modern Himalayan climbing.

Records & SportFebruary 2026

Ski Mountaineering At The Olympics

Ski mountaineering, or "skimo", made its Olympic debut at the 2026 Winter Games in Milan and Cortina, Italy.

Read more

Athletes race uphill on skis with climbing skins, run sections with skis on their backs and then ski back down. Its inclusion has brought new attention to mountain sports and to avalanche safety.
